Title:
Palabra Mark: binding an AI agent's phone call to its registered intent with a keyed, zero-bit audio mark

Authors:
Ernesto Cohnen de la C\'amara, Francisco Novella Fletcher, Jos\'e Miguel \'Avila G\'omez, Carlos Tom\'as Ferrer

Abstract (1808 characters; the limit is 1920; shortened from the paper's abstract):
AI voice agents now place phone calls on behalf of people, and nothing in a call
lets the answering side check who the agent speaks for: caller ID can be spoofed
and a voice can be cloned. In Palabra the caller registers a signed call intent
before dialling, and the speech of the call carries a near-inaudible mark
derived from a per-call secret. The receiver looks the intent up and asks one
question of the audio: does it carry this call's code? The mark is therefore a
zero-bit watermark, and the answer is a score that grows as the caller talks.
The mark shifts the loudness of 32 ms by 190 Hz tiles of the voice by up to 5 dB
in a keyed checkerboard, changing only what speech codecs keep. At a pass mark
where the false-alarm probability per wrong code is provably below 1.1e-7, it
was verified within 5 seconds on every held-out recording through AMR 12.2, AMR-
WB 12.65 and Opus 12 kbit/s, and within 3 seconds through G.711. The hardest
lines (AMR 4.75, Opus 6 kbit/s) needed 5 to 8 seconds and missed 8% of calls. No
wrong code passed in more than 400,000 trials. A small neural network in the
detector, which never sees the code and so cannot weaken the bound, cuts the
median time to verify by about 40%; it runs as the call arrives and uses no
audio from the future. AudioSeal, a leading neural watermark, fails on most of
the same calls below about 8 kbit/s. A streaming detector that never delays the
call verified all 82 marked greetings on live calls over the public phone
network, 76 of them within 3 seconds, and rejected unmarked greetings, marks
registered by another caller and replays. With the network deployed, all 14
marked greetings on new live calls were verified within 1.3 seconds of speech.
Mean PESQ-WB was 4.44, and four listeners reported no artefacts on more than 50
calls.
